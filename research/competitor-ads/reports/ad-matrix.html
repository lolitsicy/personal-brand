Creator chorus

WarmBPartnership ads

Why it works

Arrae launched with three headlines across dozens of creator ads in 48 hours. Hold the message constant and vary the face. Meta finds which creator fits which audience.

Creator hooks (to camera)

  1. I joined a waitlist for a supplement. Let me explain.
  2. The first supplement that looks like it belongs on my vanity.
  3. Biohacking, but something I'd actually post.

Script beats (20–25s)

  • 0–3s: hook, bottle in hand
  • 3–12s: "I've tried supplements. None felt like me."
  • 12–20s: bottle on chrome tray, capsules, the details
  • 20–25s: "The first edition is limited. Join with me."

Fixed copy across every creator

Headline: Join the Metabolic Reset waitlist
[Creator] is on the list. The first edition is limited. Join the waitlist.

Grade B because creators drift. No results, no "it changed my energy", nothing about their body. They haven't used it long enough to claim anything, and testimonials can't imply efficacy. Paid partnership label required. Approve every script before filming.

How to run it

The shoot delivers final images around 22 November. Don't wait for it to start learning, and don't fake the product before it arrives.

Phase 0 · now to 22 Nov

Test the words

  • Concepts 03, 04, 05 as type-led statics on generated sets, no bottle
  • 3 concepts × 2 hooks = 6 ads
  • Goal: find the hook with the lowest cost per waitlist sign-up

Phase 1 · from 23 Nov

Add the shoot

  • Winning hooks go onto real photography for 01, 02, 03, 05, 08
  • 5 concepts × 2 hooks = 10 ads, 4:5 and 9:16 each
  • Turn off anything at 2–3× target cost per sign-up with no sign-ups

Phase 2 · December

Faces and follow-up

  • 07 with 3–5 approved creators, same headline on every ad
  • 09 to 30-day site visitors and 60-day engagers, excluding the waitlist
  • 06 only once ingredients are public

Before anything goes live

  1. The brand rules still describe the old positioning. The strategist and copy guides say "clinical editorial" and warm restraint. This matrix uses the new chrome direction while keeping their claim rules. Update those guides so future work doesn't drift back.
  2. "Biohacking" may skew your audience. The word attracts male optimisation audiences, while your ICP is women 25–45 who are tired of supplements overpromising. Concepts 03 and 05 test the word directly. If 05 delivers to the wrong people, keep the look and drop the word.
  3. FON's creative. We have their headlines, not their visuals. Open the three FON examples above. If they already use chrome or lab-glass imagery, 03 and 05 need a harder fashion edge to stay distinct.
  4. Ingredients: are you ready to name ginger, green tea and barberry in ads? The shoot is built around them, but the waitlist rules say not yet.
  5. Dose, founding pricing and first-run size. Concepts 08 and 09 depend on them.
