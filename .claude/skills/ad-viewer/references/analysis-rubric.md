# Ad teardown rubric

Write `analysis.md` in this order, then `analysis.json` (schema in `ad-schema.md`). Base every point on something you can see in a frame, read in the copy or hear in the transcript, and cite it (`frame 01_001.5s`, `body line 2`, `transcript 0:04`). If a piece is missing (no transcript, media failed to download), say so and don't infer it.

## 1. Snapshot
One line: advertiser, format, length, placements, days running, active or not. Days running is the closest public proxy for performance: 30+ days active usually means it's working; under 7 tells you nothing yet. Say which this is.

## 2. Hook (0 to 3 s, or the first thing the eye lands on in a static)
- What's on screen at 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0 s (frames are sampled there on purpose).
- Hook type: problem call-out, POV, object reveal, bold claim, question, pattern interrupt, social proof, founder to camera, UGC.
- On-screen text, verbatim. Is the hook readable with the sound off?
- Score 0 to 5 for stopping power, with the reason.

## 3. Structure and flow
Beat by beat with timestamps (hook → problem → mechanism → product → proof → offer → CTA), or for statics, the reading order. Note pacing (cuts per second from the frame strip), where the product first appears, and the end card.

## 4. Copy
- Primary text: quote the first 125 characters (what shows before "more") and grade it on its own.
- Headline, link description, CTA button.
- Voice: case, length, punctuation, words that repeat. Compare to Arrae (hook, stacked benefits, ↓) and rhode (object first, lowercase, few words) where it helps.
- Claims, listed verbatim, each tagged: structure/function, implied outcome, testimonial, number/statistic, comparative.

## 5. Visual language
Set, light, palette, typography, product styling, people (who, how cast), texture. How much of the frame is product vs. lifestyle vs. type. Name the shot types so they map to our shoot list (Chrome Platter, Under the Glass, Gradient Backdrop, Product & Essentials, Inside the bag, tweezers, mirror).

## 6. Audio
Voiceover (quote the transcript's key lines), music vibe, whether it works muted.

## 7. Offer and funnel
What it asks for (buy, quiz, waitlist, bundle, discount), the landing link, urgency devices, price signals.

## 8. Compliance read (NZ/AU supplements)
Flag anything we could not say: therapeutic claims (cure, treat, prevent, detox, weight loss, fat burning), timeframes, before/after, undisclosed creator content, unsubstantiated numbers. Rate risk low / medium / high. This is about what we can borrow, not a legal opinion on them.

## 9. What Metabolic Reset should steal and avoid
- Steal: 2 to 4 specific, transferable moves (a structure, a framing, a shot, a line pattern), each rewritten as a compliant Metabolic Reset version in our voice (chrome, ice blue, "biohacking made fashionable", "formulated to support metabolism, blood sugar balance and gut health").
- Avoid: what doesn't fit our brand or our claims.
- If it's worth testing, name the campaign it fits (C1 hook test, C2 object, C3 problem to fix, C4 ritual/creators, C5 first edition, L1 to L4 launch).

## 10. Verdict
Two sentences: why this ad works or doesn't, and the single most useful lesson.

## Comparing several ads
When the library holds several ads from one brand, add `research/competitor-ads/library/_reports/<brand>-<date>.md`: the patterns across the longest-running ads (hooks, formats, lengths, offers), what they keep testing, and a ranked list of moves for us.
